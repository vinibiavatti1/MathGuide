Natual Numbers
Natural numbers are the most basic number system, representing the counting numbers used in everyday 
life. They are the set of whole numbers starting from 0 and increasing infinitely.

ℕ = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}

Properties
- Infinite: there is no largest natural number.
- Discrete: there is no natural number between two consecutive natural numbers, such as 1 and 2.
- Closed under addition and multiplication: adding or multiplying two natural numbers always 
  produces a natural number.
- Not closed under subtraction or division: 2 − 5 and 1 / 3 do not produce natural numbers.

Examples
The number of students in a classroom, the number of pages in a book, or the result of counting any 
collection of objects are all natural numbers.

0, 1, 2, 3, 42, 1000

Note
Some definitions exclude 0 from the natural numbers.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Integers
Integers extend the natural numbers by including negative whole numbers, forming a number system 
that is closed under subtraction.

ℤ = {..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}

Properties
- Infinite in both directions: there is no largest or smallest integer.
- Discrete: there is no integer between two consecutive integers, such as 1 and 2.
- Closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication: operating two integers always produces an 
  integer.
- Not closed under division: 1 / 3 does not produce an integer.
- Every natural number is an integer: ℕ ⊂ ℤ.

Examples
A debt of -200, a temperature of -5°C, or a count of 42 items are all represented by integers.

..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...

Note
The symbol ℤ comes from the German word "Zahlen", meaning "numbers".

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rational Numbers
Rational numbers extend the integers by including all numbers that can be expressed as a fraction of 
two integers, where the denominator is not zero.

ℚ = {p/q | p ∈ ℤ, q ∈ ℤ, q ≠ 0}

Properties
- Closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication and division: operating two rational numbers 
  always produces a rational number (except division by zero).
- Dense: between any two rational numbers there is always another rational number.
- Every integer is a rational number: any integer n can be written as n/1, so ℤ ⊂ ℚ.

Examples
A half, a quarter, and a repeating decimal are all rational numbers.

1/2, 3/4, -5/3, 0.333...

Note
The symbol ℚ comes from the word "quotient".

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Irrational Numbers
Irrational numbers are numbers that cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers. Their decimal 
representation is non-terminating and non-repeating.

ℝ \ ℚ = {x | x ∈ ℝ, x ∉ ℚ}

Properties
- Non-terminating and non-repeating: their decimal expansion goes on forever without a repeating 
  pattern.
- Cannot be expressed as a fraction: there are no integers p and q such that x = p/q.
- Dense: between any two real numbers there is always an irrational number.
- Irrational numbers are real numbers: ℝ \ ℚ ⊂ ℝ.

Examples
The most well-known irrational numbers are square roots of non-perfect squares and mathematical 
constants.

√2, π, e, √3, ...

Note
Irrational numbers do not have a dedicated symbol.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Real Numbers
Real numbers are all numbers that can be found on the number line. They include all rational and 
irrational numbers, forming a complete and continuous number system.

ℝ = ℚ ∪ (ℝ \ ℚ)

Properties

- Complete: there are no gaps on the number line, every point corresponds to a real number.
- Dense: between any two real numbers there is always another real number.
- Closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication and division: operating two real numbers always 
  produces a real number (except division by zero).
- Every rational and irrational number is a real number: ℚ ⊂ ℝ and ℝ \ ℚ ⊂ ℝ.

Examples
Integers, fractions, square roots and mathematical constants are all real numbers.

-3, 1/2, √2, π, 0.333...

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Imaginary Numbers
Imaginary numbers are numbers that cannot be found on the real number line. They arise from the 
square root of negative numbers, which has no solution in ℝ.

i = √-1

Notation
Imaginary numbers are represented by the symbol i, called the imaginary unit. Any imaginary number 
is a real multiple of i.

bi, b ∈ ℝ

Properties
- Not real: imaginary numbers are not part of ℝ.
- Powers of i follow a cycle: the powers of i repeat in a cycle of 4.
- Closed under addition and multiplication: operating two imaginary numbers always produces either 
  an imaginary or a real number.

i¹=i,  i²=-1,  i³=-i,  i⁴=1

Examples
The square root of any negative number produces an imaginary number.

√-1=i,  √-4=2i,  √-9=3i

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Complex Numbers
Complex numbers extend the real numbers by combining a real part and an imaginary part into a single 
number, forming the most complete number system in this hierarchy.

z = a + bi, a ∈ ℝ, b ∈ ℝ

Notation
Complex numbers are represented by the symbol ℂ. The variable z is commonly used to denote a complex 
number, where a is the real part and b is the imaginary part.

z ∈ ℂ, Re(z)=a, Im(z)=b

Properties

- Complete number system: every number introduced so far is a complex number.
- Closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication and division: operating two complex numbers 
  always produces a complex number (except division by zero).
- Every real number is a complex number: ℝ ⊂ ℂ.
- Every imaginary number is a complex number: i ∈ ℂ.

Examples
A real number, an imaginary number, and a combination of both are all complex numbers.

3+2i,   -1+5i,   4+0i,   0+3i 

Note
When b = 0, the complex number reduces to a real number. When a = 0, it reduces to an imaginary 
number.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Set Inclusion of Number Systems
The number systems introduced so far are not isolated, Each one extends the previous, forming a 
hierarchy of nested sets where every number in a smaller set also belongs to all larger sets.

ℕ ⊂ ℤ ⊂ ℚ ⊂ ℝ ⊂ ℂ

Properties
- Natural numbers are the smallest set. Every natural number is also an integer, a rational, a real, 
  and a complex number.
- Integers extend ℕ by adding negative whole numbers.
- Rational numbers extend ℤ by adding fractions.
- Real numbers extend ℚ by adding irrational numbers.
- Complex numbers extend ℝ by adding imaginary numbers.

Examples
The number 3 can be expressed in every number system.

3 ∈ ℕ,   3 ∈ ℤ,   3 = 3/1 ∈ ℚ,   3 ∈ ℝ,   3=3+0i ∈ ℂ
