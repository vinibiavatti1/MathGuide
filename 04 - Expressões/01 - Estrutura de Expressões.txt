Terms
A term is a single mathematical unit in an expression. It can be a number, a variable, or a product 
of numbers and variables. Terms are separated from each other by addition or subtraction signs.

Expression: 4x² - 3x + 7
Terms: 4x², -3x, 7

Terminology
- Monomial: an expression consisting of a single term, such as 3x².
- Binomial: an expression consisting of two terms, such as x + 5.
- Trinomial: an expression consisting of three terms, such as x² + 3x + 2.
- Polynomial: a general expression consisting of one or more terms.
- Like terms: terms that have the same variables raised to the same exponents. Only like terms can 
  be added or subtracted.
- Unlike terms: terms that differ in their variables or exponents. They cannot be combined.

Note 
The sign preceding a term belongs to that term. In 4x² - 3x + 7, the second term is -3x, not 3x.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Coefficients
A coefficient is the numerical factor of a term. It is the number that multiplies the variable or 
variables in a term.

Expression: 4x² - 3x + 7
Coefficients: 4, 3

Properties
- Implicit coefficient of 1: when no number is written before a variable, the coefficient is 1. So 
  x = 1 ∙ x.
- Implicit coefficient of -1: similarly, -x = -1 ∙ x.
- Coefficient of a constant: a term with no variable, such as 7, is its own coefficient.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Constants
A constant is a fixed value that does not change. Unlike variables, constants represent a specific 
number and remain the same throughout an expression or equation.

Expression: 4x² - 3x + 7
Constants: 7

Types of Constants
- Numeric constants: explicit numbers in an expression, such as 7, -3, or 1/2.
- Mathematical constants: fixed values with special significance, such as π or e.
- Symbolic constants: letters used to represent a fixed but unspecified value, such as a in ax + b, 
  where a and b are treated as constants and $x$ is the variable.

Note
When a constant is not a specific number, letters from the beginning of the alphabet (a, b, c) are 
typically used to represent it.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Variables
A variable is a symbol, typically a letter, that represents an unknown or changing value in an 
expression or equation. Unlike constants, the value of a variable can vary depending on the context.

Expression: 4x² - 3x + 7
Variables: x

Terminology
- Independent variable: a variable whose value is chosen freely, typically x;
- Dependent variable: a variable whose value depends on the independent variable, typically y;
- Unknown: a variable representing a specific value that is yet to be determined, as in an equation 
  to be solved.

Properties
- Convention: letters from the end of the alphabet (x, y, z) are typically used for variables;
- Scope: the meaning of a variable depends on the context in which it is defined.
